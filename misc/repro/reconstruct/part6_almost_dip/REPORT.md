It uses exactly the model of almost.py: the first 1700 ordinates on the line, the fifteen hypothetical quadruples 0.75 ± i t_j and 0.25 ± i t_j with t_j = 20 j^(5/3) < 2000, and P(a, x) = a/(π(a² + x²)). Each grid minimum is refined to a root of the analytic derivative (brentq). Each negative one is evaluated again in 30 digit arithmetic (mpmath), and its negative interval and negative mass (quad) are recorded. (3) It does the same on the windows t_j ± 0.25 (step 0.00001) around every hypothetical ordinate of the range [0, 2100] of almost.py. This finds every negative local minimum of the whole range, for the following reason. Put u = 0.75 − α and v = α − 0.25. Then the hypothetical kernels come in pairs P(v, x) − P(u, x) with v > u > 0, and each pair is positive exactly when x² > uv.
